A Survival Guide for Intercultural Communication

Have you ever been in a conversation and felt misunderstood? Don’t worry, this is not always due to poor grammar or lack of vocabulary; it is often simply a question of Intercultural Communication.

Global communication landscape
Key intercultural dimensions
Group Culture
The group, or “we,” has priority. This leads to relationships that are based on greater loyalty and selectivity.
Individual Culture
The focus is on the individual, i.e. his/her wellbeing and individual will. The “I” and self-expression take priority.
Speaking Implicity
Here, the content of the messages is somewhat veiled. More is implied than understood. A “yes” might actually just be a polite acknowledgement.
Explicit Language
Explicit language is very direct: a yes is a yes, a no is a no.
Hierarchical Culture
Hierarchy is still very important here: communication depends on who you are speaking to and/or who is speaking to you.
Focus on Time
Culture is monochronic. We tend to prioritize a specific timeframe.
Focus on Task
Polychronic cultures focus on completing the task; the timeframe is less important, even if it means delays.
Culture of Uncertainty
People accept the unknown and changes more calmly. The rules are less strict.
Change Management
Changes can feel threatening. To counteract this, strict rules are established, increasing motivation.
Informal Culture
People accept uncertainty and change more calmly. Rules tend to be flexible and open.
